# Vocabulary eval (vocab_refs.py, vocab_eval.py)

The adaptability test fired 15 never-configured keys through the sidecar, and the shots came back thin. Before any judge gets a vote on whether a generated rifle shot is a rifle shot, it has to recognise real rifle shots cut the way the generator cuts what it makes. That is the rule from `docs/water-judge.md` and `docs/static-guard.md`, applied here to every sound type the new vocabulary touches. `scripts/bench/vocab_refs.py` builds the real reference sets on the CPU. `scripts/bench/vocab_eval.py` validates each judge on them, generates SARGE's clips for the 15 keys plus footsteps on five surfaces under four generic settings, and scores each key only with judges that passed for that type at that clip length. The ear is a cross-check on the result, and `data/_gate/listen/vocab_best.wav` exists for that.

## Reference sets

Every reference clip goes through the same tail end as a generated clip: `AudioClip.from_wav` to mono, `scipy.signal.resample_poly` to 44.1 kHz, `sarge.generators.stable_audio._extract` to the kept length, then `normalize_loudness` at `target_for("normal")` (0.10 RMS, peak ceiling 0.97). Each clip is written twice, at 0.6 s (the current 1.0 s generation) and at 1.5 s (a 2.0 s generation), under `data/refs/<set>/<length>/`. A source shorter than the generation length gets a 50 ms linear fade at its end and zero padding up to that length before `_extract`, which is the ESC-50 convention for short events and keeps every reference exactly 26460 or 66150 samples, the same as a generated clip. Loud impulsive clips land below 0.10 RMS because the peak ceiling clips their gain first; generated clips go through the same limit. The build is deterministic: a second run gave the same 1818 clips and byte-identical audio. It takes about 25 s, and it deletes wav files under `data/refs/<set>/<length>/` that a previous build wrote and this one no longer lists, so the folders always match the manifest.

`data/refs/manifest.json` lists every clip with its source path, its cluster for the bootstrap (C3GD gun, HEAR shot, Freesound uploader, ESC-50 source file, or the FoleySet uploader when FoleySet holds that ESC-50 source), its recording id, its dataset license, its source length, peak time and clipped fraction, and whether it was padded at each length. It also records every exclusion with its reason. The wav files are gitignored; the manifest is small enough to keep.

## What the datasets turned out to contain

C3GD's close mics are DAVE at oh_farm (7 m, 700 files) and the left channel of ALICE at nj_farm and ny_gravel_pit (2 m, 134 and 199 files); `microphone_locations.csv` gives each of them exactly one distance. The metadata says every clip is 1.0 s at 48 kHz, and that holds for oh_farm. Every file from nj_farm and ny_gravel_pit holds 22050 samples, 0.459 s at 48 kHz, on every mic at both events. A 22050 count smells like a file written at 22.05 kHz with a 48 kHz header, so the spectra were checked before anything was built on them. The phone mics put their low-pass edge at the same place at all three events: the 20 to 21 kHz band sits 23.3 dB below the 19 to 20 kHz band on the Pixel at oh_farm, 20.7 dB below it on the Samsung tablet at nj_farm and 23.1 dB at ny_gravel_pit. If those files held 22.05 kHz audio under a 48 kHz header, the tablet's edge would sit at 9.2 kHz of real frequency, an odd place for the low-pass of a device that records at 48 kHz. The DJI mics show the same step near 16 kHz at all three events too. The rate is right and the clips are short, so the 157 ALICE shots in the gunshot sets are padded at 0.6 s, and every C3GD clip is padded at 1.5 s.

The 2 m ALICE shots are also heavily clipped. 97 percent of the ny_gravel_pit clips and 88 percent of the nj_farm clips have more than 1 percent of their samples at full scale (medians 2.3 and 4.0 percent), against a median of 0.11 percent and no clip above 1 percent for DAVE at 7 m. They stay in the sets because a real close gunshot often looks like that, and `real_by_stratum` in the validity JSON splits every judge's real rate by event and mic so a judge that only hears DAVE is visible. The pistol set follows platforms.csv to the letter, which puts the HK MR556, a 5.56 mm pistol with an 11.5 inch barrel, among the handguns. The nj_farm_2 event has spectral centroids of 5 to 10 kHz at 15 to 84 m, which looks like the clipper's 7 kHz high-pass baked into the audio; its nearest mic is 15 m away, so nothing here uses it.

FoleySet needed more curation than its sub-category names suggest. Magazine is one Joao_Janz pack of 114 clips tagged book, paper, pen, cover and writing, so it is about paper magazines and stays out. GunMechanism has 28 clips and only 15 are real firearms by the uploaders' descriptions; the other 13 are a Nerf gun, a play gun, airsoft guns (three uploads), a replica M14, the cover of a remote, stapler springs, a Baikal MP-654K standing in for a shotgun shell, a designed effect, a processed effect with no source named, a "retractable weapon" and a clip described as "gun mechanism or something". Six clips across GlassBreak and SingleStep come from FALL-E, a generative model, and are excluded. Sword is the weak one: of the 48 one-shots, the tags and descriptions mark 26 as clashes, 12 as draws, 2 as both, 1 as a swing, and leave 7 without any of these words. It is the closest real set FoleySet has for `swing|sword`, and it is labelled an approximation everywhere it appears.

The same reading of tags and descriptions turned up wrong objects and designed composites in seven more sub-categories, and `NOT_TYPE` in `vocab_refs.py` drops each one by Freesound id with its reason. PunchHit loses a zipper recording and eleven designed punches: layered vendor effects, a whipped glove over a kick drum, a cartoon punch rebuilt from egg shells, several sandbag drops summed into one hit, and similar composites. Foley punches on pillows, lettuce and sandbags stay, since that is how recorded punch effects get made. GlassBreak loses a porcelain mug, a pack of plates and mugs, tiles, a falling cup, a mixed glass and ceramics file and a glass slip mixed with a synthetic tone. ButtonPress loses a ring binder, a nail clipper, a squeezed plastic bottle, a designed menu sound, two files with no named source and a clip tagged as footsteps. DoorOpenClose loses an ice-maker lid and a train door that lets in a call to prayer, DoorSqueak loses a set of squeaking household objects with no door named and a rocking-chair creak, and DoorSlam loses a fist punching a door and two files that leave the source unclear. Splash loses the three slices of "Wet footsteps!", a recording that is also an ESC-50 footsteps clip and would otherwise sit among the negatives of every footstep judge.

FoleySet sets use one-shots only when at least 30 one-shots from at least 10 uploaders exist (Sword, PunchHit, MouseButtonClick, GlassBreak, SingleStep); the rest keep multi-shot files too, and `_extract` takes the loudest event window from them. DoorOpenClose keeps only the 24 clips whose tags and description mention opening and never closing, shutting or slamming, because the loudest window of an open-and-close recording is usually the close. DoorSqueak gets the same closing test without the opening requirement, since a bare squeak names no direction, and it drops 55 of 110 clips, among them "A metal door been slamed", two clips described as "close door" and a car door "closed with a medium slam". Each uploader contributes at most 12 clips per set and each gun at most 20 shots, picked evenly in file order, so no single uploader or gun carries a rate. In SingleStep the 12 clips of an uploader are spread over that uploader's surfaces, named surfaces first, so Joao_Janz gives 7 wood and 5 stone steps and none of the 148 dirt-and-gravel or dirt-and-grass ones.

ESC-50 and FoleySet both come from Freesound, and 18 ESC-50 clips in these classes share their Freesound source with a FoleySet file. Three of them are the same recording as a FoleySet clip of the same type (two glass breaks and a mouse click) and are dropped. The other 15 stay and take the FoleySet uploader as their bootstrap cluster, so the ESC-50 window breaks from gevaroy and the FoleySet window breaks from gevaroy count as one source.

SingleStep surfaces come from original-tags: a clip gets a surface when its tags name exactly one surface group. Wood (39 clips, 7 uploaders), stone and concrete (18, 5) and grass (15, 8) pass the bar of 8 clips from 3 uploaders. Gravel has 8 clips from 2 uploaders and metal has none, so `footstep|gravel` and `footstep|metal` are judged as footsteps but get no material check. The 96 one-shots tagged with both dirt and gravel (92 of them from Joao_Janz) are ambiguous by this rule; ambiguous and untagged steps count toward the footstep set as a whole and never toward a surface.

Two keys have no real reference anywhere in these datasets: `equip|rifle` (a weapon being drawn) and `cast|fire_spell`. They are generated and given Audiobox and shape numbers, and CLAP's identity guess is printed for information, but nothing scores them. `explosion|grenade` (fireworks), `horn|truck` (car horn) and `swing|sword` (clashes and draws) are scored against approximate references and flagged.

## Judges

AST and PANNs run through `water_judge.run_ast` and `run_panns` with the four fits of `recalibrate.fit` (none, silence, tile, tile_floor), CLAP through `ab_clap.clap_scores` at its native fit, and Audiobox through `validate_panel.judge_audiobox` in chunks. One model is on the GPU at a time, with `gc` and `empty_cache` between them, and the generator is freed before the first judge loads.

AudioSet label sets are per type, resolved by name against the label list the loaded model reports, and the run exits on any missing name. Both lists (AST `config.json` and PANNs `class_labels_indices.csv`) hold the same 527 names in the same order. Two names from the request do not exist in AudioSet: "Mouse click" and "Punch". The nearest real classes are Clicking and the hit classes Slap, smack; Whack, thwack; Thump, thud; Thunk. "Mouse" exists but is the rodent, so it is left out. The sets:

CLAP gets two to four phrasings per type (for example "a rifle gunshot", "a rifle firing" and "a gunshot"). Each phrase is scored as a softmax over itself plus a distractor list made of seven generic phrases ("white noise static", "wind", "fire crackling", "water dripping", "a knock", "music", "speech"), the two unvalidated key phrases ("a weapon being drawn", "a magic fire spell"), and the canonical phrases of every type in another family. `any` pools all of a type's phrases. The multi-class identity test puts the canonical phrases of every type (the adapt_test wording where one exists, plus "an empty gun clicking" for the mechanism set and "swords clashing" and "a sword being drawn" for the sword set, which is mostly clashes and draws) into a single softmax with the extras and the generic phrases: `id` counts a hit when the argmax is a canonical phrase of that type, `idfam` when it is any phrase of the same family, so pistol versus rifle confusion shows up in `id` and costs nothing in `idfam`. "A weapon being drawn" describes a sword draw correctly, so it is left out of the sword distractors and counts toward the sword family in `idfam`. The material test is a separate 5-way softmax over "the sound of a footstep on wood / stone / grass / metal / gravel", checked first on the real FoleySet single steps per surface. That is the material positive control the footstep gate never had: a generated surface counts only where CLAP names the real steps of that surface at least half the time and gives that surface to other surfaces' steps at least 0.30 less often.

## Validity rule

A variant (model, fit, label set or phrase, rule) votes on a type at a length when the real rate is at least 0.50, the real rate minus the pooled negative rate is at least 0.30, and the real rate minus the synthetic hiss rate is at least 0.30. Each length is checked separately. The negatives for a type are every real set from another family (gunshot, gun_mech, shell, explosion, sword, door, glass, click, punch, horn, dog, water, footstep, fire, wind, knock), sampled evenly to at most 40 clips per family so the 608-clip gunshot family never drowns out the 40-clip horn family; the gun_mech and shell families hold 15 and 39. Same-family sets are never negatives, so a rifle is never a false alarm for a pistol judge and a door slam never counts against a door-opening judge. The hiss is the 40 synthetic static clips of `water_judge.hiss` (four spectra, ten seeds), cut the same way. `top` counts a hit when a label of the set is in the AudioSet top 5 or a phrase of the group is the CLAP argmax; `thr` thresholds the summed probability at the 90th percentile of the pooled negatives.

Every rate carries a 95 percent interval from 2000 bootstrap draws over independent sources: guns for C3GD, shots for HEAR, uploaders for FoleySet, source files for ESC-50 (or the FoleySet uploader where FoleySet holds the source), seeds for the hiss and for generated clips. A variant marked F also has the lower bounds of its real rate and of its gap past the thresholds, and a strict variant keeps the 0.30 gap against every negative family taken alone. Per type and length the script keeps one usable variant per model family, preferring F, then strict, then the pooled AUC, then the gap. Picking the best of many variants on the same real clips flatters the winner a little, as in the water judge.

## Scoring the keys

Generation uses `StableAudio3Generator(sampler_type="euler", steps=3)`, which is the sidecar's default tier with every other production default (cfg 2.0, fp16, accelerator and prompt hoist, 1.0 s padding, static guard with two retries), intensity normal, and seeds 0 to 19 through `seed_for`. The four settings change only real generator attributes: `steps` and `detail_steps` (3 or 8), and `gen_duration_s` with `duration_s` (1.0 and 0.6, or 2.0 and 1.5). The prompts stay `build_prompt` output, and the script stops if any prompt or per-key step count differs. `s3_g1.0_k0.6` is the current setting. Clips go to `data/_gate/vocab/<setting>/<event>-<material>_NN.wav` and are judged from those files; `--reuse` skips generation when all of them exist and then never loads the model. A 2.0 s generation changes the latent shape, so the CUDA graph captures a new entry, the hoist opens a new slot (both key on tensor shapes) and the conditioning cache adds a new (prompt, seconds) entry. The log line after each setting prints the accelerator and hoist counters (conditioning misses, graphs, replays, eager calls, capture failures, slots, slot failures, static retries) so a fallback to eager shows up, and the JSON keeps them under `meta.generator.fast_stats`. After the generator and after each judge the log prints the allocated and reserved GPU memory, which should fall back near zero before the next model loads. The script also stops at start-up when the manifest was built at another sample rate, intensity or pair of kept and generated lengths.

For each key and setting, every judge kept for that type at that length gives a rate over the 20 seeds and a ratio to the real rate that judge measured at that length. The consensus is the footstep gate's: the minimum over the kept judges of min(ratio, 1), with an interval from a joint bootstrap that resamples seeds and real sources together across judges. The best setting is the highest consensus, then the highest mean ratio, then the current setting. The table says whether any setting reaches 0.8 of real and whether one does with its lower bound past 0.8. Where the best and current settings share a judge variant, a McNemar test paired on seed compares them, marked x across the two lengths. Beside the judges, the script compares six shape features against the real set at the same length (time within 30 dB of the peak, level 100 ms after the peak, share of energy below 500 Hz, crest factor, 10 to 90 percent attack time, and the noise floor as the 10th percentile 10 ms frame with digital silence left out) as the share of generated clips inside the real 10 to 90 percent range, and reports Audiobox PQ against the real PQ. Footsteps compare against the real steps of their surface where one exists.

`data/_gate/listen/vocab_best.wav` plays, per key, three seeds at the current setting and then three at the best setting; keys with no judged setting play the current setting alone, and the log prints the order. Everything lands in `scripts/bench/out_vocab_eval.json`.

## Validity results so far

None. Every judge here is a neural model that runs on the GPU, so no validity number exists until the GPU run. The CPU work settled the reference sets above and the dataset problems in them; it says nothing yet about which judges hear a rifle.

## Run command

The GPU run is roughly 75 minutes on the 3060, so start it detached. The estimate scales the per-clip costs in `recalibrate.log` (AST 28 s per fit and CLAP 282 s for 532 clips) to 5316 clips (3636 references, 80 hiss, 1600 generated): CLAP about 47 minutes, AST about 19 for four fits, generation, PANNs and Audiobox a few minutes each. It goes through `runpinned.py`, which pins the process off core 8:

```powershell
Start-Process -FilePath D:\Python\SAGE\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -ArgumentList '-u','D:\Python\sarge-research\modal\runpinned.py','D:\Python\SAGE\scripts\bench\vocab_eval.py' -WorkingDirectory D:\Python\SAGE -RedirectStandardOutput D:\Python\SAGE\scripts\bench\vocab_eval.log -RedirectStandardError D:\Python\SAGE\scripts\bench\vocab_eval.err -WindowStyle Hidden -PassThru
```

The run is finished when `vocab_eval.log` ends with `saved -> D:\Python\SAGE\scripts\bench\out_vocab_eval.json`. Extra flags go after the script path: `--reuse` rescores existing clips without the generator, `--judges ast panns` gives a 25 minute run, and `--tag-fits tile none` halves AST. To rebuild the references, which only the manifest and wav files depend on:

```powershell
D:\Python\SAGE\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -u D:\Python\sarge-research\modal\runpinned.py D:\Python\SAGE\scripts\bench\vocab_refs.py
```

## Dry run

`vocab_dry\run_dry.py` in the session scratchpad runs `vocab_eval.main` end to end on the real reference files with torch, torchaudio, transformers, panns_inference, audiobox_aesthetics and stable_audio_3 blocked at the import system. `validate_panel`, `recalibrate` and `ab_clap` are stubbed by lifting their real functions out of the source with `ast` (`_resample` swapped for a scipy version); `water_judge` imports for real. The judges are fakes seeded from each clip's bytes, built to make some types valid, some invalid, and to make AST call the 0.6 s hiss a gunshot. The generator is a subclass of the real `StableAudio3Generator` whose model returns decaying noise, so `_steps_for`, `_prompt`, `_inputs` and `_finish` are the real code. All 31 checks pass in under a minute: 3636 reference clips load; 401 model calls per setting at the right steps and duration; 20 prompts identical to `build_prompt`; production sampler, cfg and padding; 1600 wav files at 26460 or 66150 samples; the generator, then each judge, loads and frees in turn; no negative pool contains the family under test; every real rate sits inside its interval; all 24 AST gunshot variants that call hiss a gunshot fail the hiss check and none is chosen; the types with no valid judge, the two keys with no reference and the three approximations are flagged; the material control runs for wood, stone and grass while metal and gravel stay unjudged; the listening file is written; both real label lists resolve every name; and a `--reuse` pass loads no generator and reproduces every consensus number. Every judge number in the dry-run log comes from the fakes and means nothing.

## Known weaknesses

The thinnest controls are the ones the user complained about most. Gun mechanisms rest on 15 clips from 9 uploaders, shotguns on 5 guns and punches on 58 clips from 19 uploaders, so their intervals will be wide and a judge can pass or fail on a couple of sources. Reloads, empty clicks and the rifle equip are three different events, and only the first two share the mechanism set. The sword set is clashes and draws, so a valid sword judge says a clip sounds like a blade, which is a weaker claim than "a swing". Fireworks and car horns stand in for grenades and truck horns. The 2 m gunshots are short and clipped, which is why the strata split exists, and the 1.5 s gunshot references are 1.0 s or 0.46 s of shot followed by silence, where a real 1.5 s window would carry more tail and room. A generated 1.5 s shot with a long natural tail can therefore look less like these references on the tail feature while sounding better, so read the tail column beside the judges at 1.5 s.
