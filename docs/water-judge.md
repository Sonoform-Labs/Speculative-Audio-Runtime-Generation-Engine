# Water judge panel (water_judge.py)

`water_judge.py` asks one question before it scores a single generated clip: does this judge recognise real water at the clip length it will be asked to judge? The positive control is the 40 ESC-50 `water_drops` clips, cut the way the generator cuts its output (`sarge.generators.stable_audio._extract` onset window, then `normalize_loudness` at `target_for("normal")`, 0.10 RMS) at 0.6 s, 1.0 s and 1.5 s, plus the untouched 5 s files. The negatives are the 200 clips of `crackling_fire`, `wind`, `door_wood_knock`, `glass_breaking` and `footsteps`, cut the same way and reported per class. A third control, 40 synthetic static clips, stands in for the failure the gate exists to catch (see "Static check" below). A judge that fails the real control at a given length, or calls the static clips water, never scores generated clips of that length.

## Run command

The run is about 27 minutes on the 3060, so start it detached. The estimate comes from `recalibrate.log` (AST 28 s and CLAP 282 s per fit for 532 clips) scaled to 2040 clips: AST about 7 minutes for four fits, PANNs about 1 minute, CLAP about 18 minutes for the native fit. CLAP dominates because the transformers 5.8.1 `ClapFeatureExtractor` builds the mel spectrogram frame by frame in a Python loop (`audio_utils.spectrogram`).

The command goes through `D:\Python\sarge-research\modal\runpinned.py`, which sets the process affinity to `0xEFF`, reads it back and refuses to run while core 8 is enabled (core 8 on this machine throws machine-check reboots under load). The runner changes into the script's folder before it starts the script; `water_judge.py` then falls back to `D:\Python\SAGE\scripts\bench` and resolves every data path from the repo root, so the working directory has no effect on the result.

## Label sets

AST (`id2label` in `config.json` of MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593, identical in F:\AI\cache\hf and D:\hf_cache) and PANNs (`C:\Users\alanc\panns_data\class_labels_indices.csv`) carry the same 527 AudioSet names in the same order. The dry run checked both lists and resolved every name below against each. At run time the script resolves names against the list the loaded model reports and exits with an error on any missing name.

`water_all`, 23 classes, every water or liquid class in the list apart from the exclusions below:

- Water (288): the generic class a tagger reaches for when the water subtype is unclear.
- Rain (289), Raindrop (290), Rain on surface (291): falling water. Raindrop is the closest AudioSet class to one ESC-50 drop. Rain and Rain on surface are broadband, which makes them the likeliest classes to fire on wind or static.
- Stream (292), Waterfall (293), Ocean (294), Waves, surf (295): moving bodies of water, the setting of a body landing in a pond or stream. Same broadband risk.
- Gurgling (297): bubbling water; the current prompt asks for "bubbling".
- Liquid (444): the generic liquid class.
- Splash, splatter (445), Slosh (446), Plop (494): the target events, something hitting or stirring a water surface.
- Drip (448): drops, the texture of the control itself.
- Pour (449), Trickle, dribble (450), Gush (451), Fill (with liquid) (452): liquid flow, the texture of a splash tail.
- Boiling (456): bubbling liquid.
- Water tap, faucet (370), Sink (filling or washing) (371), Bathtub (filling or washing) (372), Toilet flush (374): household running water.

Left out on purpose: Steam (296) and Spray (453) are hiss, the texture of the static failure itself. Squish (447) also describes muddy footsteps, so the footsteps negatives would leak into it. Pump (liquid) (454) and Stir (455) are machine and utensil sounds, Gargling (56) is a throat sound, the boat classes (301 to 304) are engines, and Tap (360) and Sine wave (501) only match the search by substring.

`liquid`, 13 classes: `water_all` minus the ambience classes (Rain, Rain on surface, Stream, Waterfall, Ocean, Waves, surf) and the four plumbing classes. Those ten are the ones a tagger can plausibly hand to wind or hiss, so this set is the safer broad set for the static question.

`splash`, 3 classes: Splash, splatter; Slosh; Plop. The narrow splash-only set.

`drip`, 2 classes: Drip; Raindrop. Diagnostic only. It is printed and saved and never ranked, and it shows how much of a judge's pass on the drop control comes from drip labels alone.

## CLAP phrasings

Each water phrase is scored as a separate softmax over [phrase] plus the distractors: "water splashing", "something dropping into water", "a splash", "water", "liquid sloshing". `any` pools all five against the distractors and counts a hit when any of them wins. "water dripping" is a diagnostic phrase with the same role as the `drip` set. Distractors: "white noise static", "wind", "fire crackling", "a footstep", "a knock", "an explosion", "glass breaking", "music", "speech". "rain" is left out of the distractors on purpose. The probe's list (`tier_probe.WATER_TEXTS`) kept it as a competitor, and rain is water, so it splits probability away from the water phrase on any drop-like clip. That choice cuts the other way on the broken clips: the probe's CLAP put "rain" first on 7 to 23 of the 40 L1 clips per config, and without it their probability has to land somewhere else, possibly on "water". The static check below catches exactly that case, since a CLAP variant that calls hiss water stops scoring generated clips. All texts go through one forward pass, and each set takes its columns from the shared logits, which is valid because CLAP logits are per text. CLAP runs the native fit by default; the processor config pads with `repeatpad`, so native already tiles to the 10 s window.

## Validity rule

The rule is the one in `validate_panel.anchor` and `recalibrate.analyze`. A variant (model | fit | label set | rule) is valid at a length when the real water rate is at least 0.50 and the real rate minus the pooled negative rate is at least 0.30. It is checked separately at 0.6 s, 1.0 s, 1.5 s and on the full 5 s files. The table marks `Y` when the 0.30 gap also holds against every negative class taken alone, `y` for a pooled-only pass, `-` for a failure. That stricter mark matters here: a pooled negative rate of 10 percent can hide wind at 40 percent, and wind and fire are the real sounds closest to the static failure. Each row also prints the AUC of real water against the pooled negatives and against wind plus fire (`AUCnz`); per-class AUCs are in the JSON.

## Static check

The ESC-50 negatives have no stationary hiss in them, and hiss is the way this generator fails: the 84 clips the probe's static rule flags (mostly L1) have a median long-term flatness of 0.41, onset rise 4.7 dB, centroid 4.3 kHz and 21 percent of their energy below 500 Hz. Wind and fire are the nearest ESC-50 classes, and both are gusty or crackling where the generator's static is steady broadband noise. An AudioSet tagger can put Rain, Stream or Waterfall in the top 5 for hiss, and all three sit in `water_all`. A judge could pass the real control on ESC-50 negatives, call static water, and then rank the static-heavy L1 configs (and their crop06 sets in the 0.6 s window) at the top. The CLAP list in the probe gave the L1 clips "rain" as top-1 on 7 to 23 of 40 per config, and this panel drops "rain" from the CLAP distractors, so the risk applies to CLAP as well.

`water_judge.py` therefore builds 40 synthetic static clips, 5 s long, 10 seeds for each of four spectra written as (slope of the power spectrum, high-pass in Hz): white (0, 50), (0.5, 50), high-passed pink (1.0, 200) and (1.25, 400). High-passed pink matches the probe's static clips most closely (flatness 0.52, rise 4.7 dB, centroid 4.6 kHz, low500 0.21); the other three spread the colour either side. They go through the same `_extract` onset window and `normalize_loudness` at every length as the real control. The dry run confirms the probe's static rule flags 100 percent of them at every length, against 0 percent of the real water, fire, wind, knock and footstep clips. The spectra were picked from the probe's static-rule summary statistics alone; no judge score of any kind went into them.

The rule: a variant is usable at a length when it is valid (the validate_panel rule above, unchanged, on ESC-50 negatives only) and the real water rate minus the hiss rate is at least the same 0.30 gap. Only usable variants score generated clips, in the printout, in the JSON and in the per-clip dump. The validity table prints a hiss column for both rules, an AUC of real water against hiss (`AUChs`), the top-1 labels each tagger gives the hiss, and marks a valid variant that fails this check with `h`. Each window lists the valid variants that the hiss check removed.

## Fits and rules

Fits for AST and PANNs are none, silence and tile (the modes of `validate_panel.fit_length`) plus tile_floor (tile with a 1e-3 noise floor). The script calls `recalibrate.fit` for all four, which matches `fit_length` on the first three. Two rules run per set. `top` counts a clip as water when any label of the set is in the top 5 (AST, PANNs) or a water phrase is the CLAP argmax. `thr` sums the set's probabilities and thresholds at the 90th percentile of the pooled negatives at that same length. Because `thr` takes its threshold from the negatives, its pooled negative rate sits near 10 percent by construction, so for `thr` the rule reduces to a real rate of at least 0.50, and the AUC column carries the discrimination evidence.

## Scoring and ranking

Every generated set is matched to the control of its clip length: L0, every `_crop06` set and the three tier sets at 0.6 s, L1 at 1.0 s, L2 at 1.5 s. Variants that fail the control or the static check at that length never touch the set, in the printout or in the JSON. Per length the script keeps one variant per model family (strict pass first, then highest AUC, then largest gap), ranks the sets by the footstep gate's consensus, min over the kept variants of min(rate / real rate, 1), and breaks ties by the mean ratio and then the static rate. Each cell carries a paired McNemar against `L0_1.0_0.6|current`, matched on key and seed from the `event-material_NN.wav` names (an `x` marks a pair across two windows, where the judge has two different sensitivities), plus a second McNemar against the current wording at the same generation length. When no non-diagnostic variant is usable at a length, the log prints `NO VALID JUDGE at <len> s` and ranks nothing there. The static column recomputes the probe rule with `water_probe.metrics`; if that import fails, the script falls back to the per-clip `static` fields of `out_water_probe.json` (`c_static` for crop06 sets) and says so.

## Known weaknesses

The positive control is drops, and a drop is a different event from a splash. A judge can pass the control on Drip and Raindrop and still have no model of a body landing in water. The script exposes this in four places. The `splash` set has a separate validity row, which shows whether any judge hears the drops as splash-like at all. The `drip` set and the "water dripping" phrase print as diagnostics, so a judge whose pass rests on drip labels is visible. The PER KEY table splits each kept judge's rate across splash|water, footstep|water, impact|water (heavy) and land|water, so a judge that rewards drippy footsteps and ignores splashes shows up as a lopsided row. The lead-label column names the water label that carried each hit. A low splash|water rate under a judge valid only through `water_all` or `liquid` says little about splash quality.

Forty clips is a thin control. A real rate of 50 percent carries a 95 percent interval of roughly 35 to 65 percent, so a variant near the line can flip on two clips, and picking the best-AUC variant on the same 40 clips flatters it slightly (the ratio uses that same real rate). Cross-length numbers need care too: L2 at 1.5 s and L0 at 0.6 s are scored against different controls, so the ratio to the real rate at each length is the fair comparison and the crop06 sets are the clean paired test of generation length. The judges hear the whole window, while the static rule looks at the long-term spectrum and the onset, so read the static column beside the judge rates. The full 5 s controls stay unnormalized, as in `recalibrate.py`.

## Dry run

`wf4\dry\run_dry.py` imports `water_judge` behind stub modules for `validate_panel`, `recalibrate`, `ab_clap` and `water_probe`. The stubs are built by lifting the real functions (`_resample`, `fit`, `auc`, `softmax`, `mcnemar`, `metrics` and its helpers, `crest_db`) out of the real source files with `ast`, so every numpy path is the real code. Only the three model runners are swapped for fakes that return random scores seeded from each clip's bytes; the AST fake is valid at 0.6 s, the PANNs fake at 0.6 s and 1.5 s, the CLAP fake at 0.6 s. The AST fake also puts Rain, Stream and Waterfall on 90 percent of the hiss clips at 0.6 s, the way a real tagger can, so the static check has something to catch. It ran end to end on the real files in 30 s with torch, transformers and panns_inference absent from `sys.modules`. All 25 checks in `dry_checks.txt` pass: 51 sets and 2040 clips load (4 real, 20 negative, 4 hiss, 23 generated); the probe's static rule flags every hiss clip at every length; all eight AST `water_all` variants at 0.6 s pass the real control, fail the static check with hiss rates of 90 to 97 percent, and score no generated set, while every other judge keeps its standing and the chosen AST judge moves to a `liquid` variant; every generated set lands in the window its file length implies; the judges load and free one at a time; both real label lists resolve every set name; tier `water_s3` is byte-identical to `L0_1.0_0.6|current` and McNemar between them is 0/0 on all 68 usable variants, which confirms key and seed matching; the recomputed static rule agrees with `out_water_probe.json` on 800 of 800 clips; the 1.0 s window prints NO VALID JUDGE and ranks nothing.

Every judge number in `dry_run.log` comes from the fakes and means nothing. The static numbers are real, since the static rule ran on the real audio. It flags 0 percent of real water drops, fire, wind, knocks and footsteps at every length and 20 percent of glass breaking, so on real recordings the rule is close to silent and the 30 to 72 percent static rates on L1 belong to the generated clips.
